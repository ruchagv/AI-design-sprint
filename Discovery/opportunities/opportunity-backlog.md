## Business Objective

> **Help urban Indian corporate professionals eliminate daily food decision fatigue — so they eat well, feel good, and never have to agonise over what to eat.**

The product must surface contextually relevant food suggestions with near-zero daily input, differentiate itself from junk-like diet apps, and validate willingness to pay within a 2-week sprint.

*Source: PROJECT-OVERVIEW.md — Sprint Goal + North Star*

---

## Product Outcomes

Product outcomes are the measurable changes in user behaviour that, if achieved, would signal the business objective is being met. Each outcome is directly traceable to what participants said they want their life to look like.

---

### Outcome 1 — Users offload the daily "what should I eat?" decision

**What this means:** Users stop spending cognitive energy deciding what to eat each day. Instead, they open the product, see a suggestion ready for them, and act on it — whether that's telling their cook, ordering in, or cooking themselves.

**Why this matters:** DO-03 (hand off planning entirely) was cited by 4 of 5 participants. DO-01 (eat healthy without thinking hard) was also cited by 4 of 5. This is the dominant aspiration in the research.

**Signal of success:** Users report feeling less mental effort around food decisions on a typical weekday. They open the product before or instead of opening Swiggy/Zomato to figure out what to eat.

---

### Outcome 2 — Users regularly eat meals that are both healthy and enjoyable

**What this means:** Users are no longer stuck choosing between "good for me" and "tastes good." They discover and eat meals they genuinely enjoy that also meet their dietary and fitness goals — and build a personal repertoire of those meals over time.

**Why this matters:** PP-03 (taste vs. health conflict) was cited by 3 of 5 participants. DO-02 (meals that are tasty AND healthy) was cited by 3 of 5. P2 described cracking a great chicken fajita meal as the best food day possible.

**Signal of success:** Users report satisfaction with what they ate that day. They return to meals suggested by the product rather than defaulting to the same 3 dishes they always fall back on.

---

### Outcome 3 — Users feel quietly in control of their nutrition without obsessing over it

**What this means:** Users have a loose but reliable sense of whether their week is going well nutritionally — without logging every meal or tracking macros obsessively. They feel informed, not surveilled.

**Why this matters:** PP-06 (manual tracking too effortful) was cited by 3 of 5 — all prior apps failed because of the logging burden. DO-04 (guilt-free eating) and DO-05 (achievable nutrient plan) reflect the same underlying desire: confidence without effort.

**Signal of success:** Users describe their relationship with food as "better" or "less stressful" after a week of use. They are not gaming the system or ignoring it — they are checking in casually and feeling reassured.

## Customer Opportunities

Customer opportunities are the specific moments where users struggle, and where the product can intervene to help. Each opportunity is directly mapped to research evidence.

---

### OPP-01 — Knowing what to eat today, before the decision becomes stressful

**Opportunity statement:** When users start their day (especially on busy weekdays), they need a ready answer to "what should I eat today?" so that they can act immediately without mental effort.

**Evidence:**
- PP-02: 3/5 participants experience the morning cook-instruction burden as the hardest decision point of the day
- DO-03: 4/5 participants want someone or something to hand them the plan
- WA-01: Users who delegate to a flatmate are the least stressed — the product can replicate that relief

**Why it's an opportunity:** This is the primary daily job. If the product solves nothing else, solving this one moment — the morning "what do I eat?" — creates immediate, repeatable value.

**Linked outcome:** Outcome 1

**Research IDs:** PP-02, DO-01, DO-03, WA-01

---

### OPP-02 — Communicating today's meal to the cook without having to think it up

**Opportunity statement:** When users have a cook arriving in the morning, they need a ready, cook-friendly instruction (what dish, which ingredients) so they don't freeze up or default to the same safe meal again.

**Evidence:**
- PP-02: P1, P4, P5 all described the "cook arrives, what do I say?" moment as a recurring pain point
- PP-04: Repeated meals happen not by choice but because users can't think of alternatives on the spot
- UN-05: Users don't know which groceries to order, which means the cook is also limited by a constrained pantry

**Why it's an opportunity:** 4 of 5 participants have a cook. The "tell the cook" workflow is the primary food execution path. A suggestion the user can simply hand to the cook removes the most frequent daily friction entirely.

**Linked outcome:** Outcome 1

**Research IDs:** PP-02, PP-04, UN-05, WA-02

---

### OPP-03 — Choosing what to order on delivery apps without scrolling into decision paralysis

**Opportunity statement:** When users are hungry and have no food ready at home, they need a specific recommendation of what to order (and ideally where from) so they don't spend 20 minutes scrolling and end up ordering something unsatisfying.

**Evidence:**
- PP-01: 3/5 participants described scrolling-induced decision paralysis when ordering
- PP-08: 2/5 described the worst food day as ordering food that was neither healthy nor tasty
- WA-03: Users are already turning to AI on demand for this — a formalised version of that behaviour would be welcomed

**Why it's an opportunity:** This is the fallback scenario when the primary (home-cooked) path fails. Without solving this, the product only helps on good days. A delivery recommendation — filtered by dietary preferences and health goals — plugs the gap.

**Linked outcome:** Outcome 1

**Research IDs:** PP-01, PP-08, WA-03

---

### OPP-04 — Discovering meals that are both satisfying and aligned with health goals

**Opportunity statement:** When users are stuck in a cycle of eating the same uninspiring meals, they need curated meal ideas that are genuinely appealing and nutritionally appropriate — so they can build a repertoire they actually want to eat.

**Evidence:**
- PP-03: 3/5 participants experience an ongoing taste-vs-health tension — they currently feel forced to choose
- PP-04: 3/5 participants fall into repetitive meals purely from a lack of ideas
- DO-02: 3/5 participants described "meals that are tasty AND healthy" as what a great food week looks like
- WA-06: Users actively browse YouTube and Instagram for food ideas — they are hungry for inspiration, just not in a structured form

**Why it's an opportunity:** This is where delight lives. Users don't just want the burden removed — they want to rediscover that food can be enjoyable. A product that surfaces genuinely good meal ideas (not generic diet-plan meals) creates emotional value beyond utility.

**Linked outcome:** Outcome 2

**Research IDs:** PP-03, PP-04, DO-02, WA-06

---

### OPP-05 — Knowing how the week is going nutritionally without counting anything

**Opportunity statement:** When users reflect on their food choices, they want a lightweight signal that tells them whether they're broadly on track — without needing to log every meal or calculate macros — so they can course-correct naturally.

**Evidence:**
- PP-06: 3/5 participants abandoned tracking apps because of the logging burden
- UN-04: 2/5 participants explicitly want accountability and check-ins — they don't want to be left to self-police
- DO-04: 2/5 participants described "guilt-free" as the emotional goal — they want to feel okay about their choices
- DO-05: 2/5 fitness-focused participants want macro guidance without becoming obsessive

**Why it's an opportunity:** All tracking apps have failed these users by requiring them to do work. The opportunity is passive awareness — the product observes and summarises rather than demanding input. This is also a key differentiator from HealthifyMe / MyFitnessPal.

**Linked outcome:** Outcome 3

**Research IDs:** PP-06, UN-04, DO-04, DO-05

---

### OPP-06 — Ordering the right groceries so the week's meals are possible

**Opportunity statement:** When users place their weekly grocery order, they need a suggested list tied to the upcoming week's meals so that the right ingredients are in the fridge — and the cook (or the user) can execute without last-minute improvisation.

**Evidence:**
- UN-05: 2/5 participants cited grocery ordering decisions as a recurring friction point
- PP-02: The cook's options are limited by what's in the fridge — solving groceries unlocks better meal execution
- WA-02: P1 independently built a rotation system to manage this — the product can formalise and improve on that behaviour

**Why it's an opportunity:** Groceries are the upstream decision that determines whether all other food decisions are easy or hard. A weekly grocery suggestion connected to a meal plan closes the loop and makes the whole system self-reinforcing.

**Linked outcome:** Outcome 1 + Outcome 2

**Research IDs:** UN-05, PP-02, WA-02

## Prioritisation Notes

These opportunities are not equally weighted for the sprint. Given the 2-week timeline and prototype goal:

| Opportunity | Sprint Priority | Rationale |
|-------------|----------------|-----------|
| OPP-01 — Know what to eat today | **P0 — Must solve** | Core value proposition. Cited by 4/5 participants. Nothing else matters if this doesn't work. |
| OPP-02 — Communicate to cook | **P0 — Must solve** | 4/5 participants have a cook. This is the primary execution path for the product. |
| OPP-04 — Discover tasty+healthy meals | **P1 — High value** | Where delight and differentiation from diet apps live. Drives repeat usage. |
| OPP-03 — Delivery recommendation | **P1 — High value** | Covers the fallback scenario. Without it, the product only works on good days. |
| OPP-05 — Lightweight nutrition awareness | **P2 — Include if possible** | Important for retention and differentiation from HealthifyMe. Requires careful design to avoid becoming a tracking burden. |
| OPP-06 — Grocery suggestions | **P2 — Include if possible** | High leverage but adds scope. Consider as an optional downstream feature off OPP-02. |
