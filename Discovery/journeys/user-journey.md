# Core User Journey — Food Decision Sprint
**Persona:** Arjun, 28 — primary persona  
**Journey type:** Current state (with pain) + Future state (with product)  
**Scope:** Typical weekday — morning cook instruction through dinner  
**Date compiled:** August 2026

---

## Journey Overview

The journey covers the four food decision moments in Arjun's weekday. Each moment is a potential point of friction — or, with the product, a point of relief.

| Moment | Time | Current state | With product |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Morning cook instruction** | 7:00–7:30am | Blank, repeats yesterday | Suggestion ready, forwards to cook |
| **2. Lunch decision** | 12:30–1:00pm | Eats what's available, no thought | Home-cooked meal from morning instruction |
| **3. Evening hunger** | 4:00–5:00pm | Tea and whatever's around | Light snack, no decision needed |
| **4. Dinner** | 8:30–9:30pm | Opens Swiggy or eats cook leftovers | Cook leftovers or a guided order suggestion |

---

## Current State Journey (Without Product)

### Moment 1 — Morning cook instruction *(7:00–7:30am)*

**What happens:**
Arjun wakes up. The cook arrives at 7:15–7:30am and needs an instruction. Arjun is half-awake, hasn't thought about food, and has no plan. He checks the fridge, sees the same vegetables from last week, and either tells the cook to make the obvious dish or says "make whatever's there."

**Thinking:** *"What's in the fridge... okay, there's bindi. She can make bindi fry. Again."*

**Feeling:** Low-grade stress, mild resignation. Not acute pain — just the quiet cost of starting the day without a plan.

**Failure modes:**
- Repeats the same dish for the third time this week
- Cook gets no instruction and improvises from limited groceries
- Arjun skips the decision entirely and plans to order later

**Pain level:** ★★★★☆

---

### Moment 2 — Lunch *(12:30–1:00pm)*

**What happens:**
Arjun is at the office. He either eats what the cook packed (which is probably repetitive) or goes to the canteen. No active decision stress — he eats what's in front of him.

**Thinking:** *"Dal roti again. Fine."*

**Feeling:** Neutral to mildly unsatisfied. No guilt — this was inevitable.

**Failure modes:**
- Eats a canteen meal that is neither healthy nor satisfying
- Packs the same lunch he's had for the last 4 days

**Pain level:** ★★☆☆☆

---

### Moment 3 — Evening snack *(4:00–5:00pm)*

**What happens:**
Hunger creeps in. Arjun has chai and whatever biscuits or snacks are around. No real decision is made — it's just reflex.

**Thinking:** *"Chai time."*

**Feeling:** Fine. This moment rarely causes stress.

**Failure modes:**
- Eats junk because nothing planned is available
- Overeats at snack time because lunch was unsatisfying

**Pain level:** ★☆☆☆☆

---

### Moment 4 — Dinner *(8:30–9:30pm)*

**What happens:**
Arjun gets home. If the cook left dinner, he eats it — often the same dish as lunch, or something safe and unexciting. If it doesn't feel right, or if there's nothing, he opens Swiggy. He scrolls for 10–20 minutes. He's tired, hungry, and decision-fatigued. He orders something he thinks he wants. It often disappoints.

**Thinking:** *"I've already eaten everything on here. Fine, biryani again."*

**Feeling:** Frustration, hangry-scrolling, followed by mild guilt after eating. The worst food days cluster here.

**Failure modes:**
- Orders junk food because he can't decide anything better
- Spends 20 minutes scrolling and ends up with food that's neither healthy nor satisfying
- Feels guilty, asks ChatGPT if what he ate was okay

**Pain level:** ★★★★★

## Future State Journey (With Product)

### Moment 1 — Morning cook instruction *(7:00–7:30am)*

**What happens:**
Arjun wakes up. Before the cook arrives, he opens the app — or a notification has already surfaced the suggestion. He sees: *"Today: Palak dal + 2 rotis. Ingredients: spinach, dal, onion — all in your fridge."* He copies the message and sends it to the cook on WhatsApp.

**Thinking:** *"Done."*

**Feeling:** Relief. The decision is already made. He didn't have to think. The cook has a clear instruction and the right ingredients.

**What the product did:**
- Knew what was in Arjun's fridge from the weekly grocery profile
- Matched a meal to his protein goal and vegetarian preference
- Surfaced it before he had to ask
- Formatted it as a cook-ready instruction, not a macro breakdown

**Time spent on food decision:** Under 30 seconds.

---

### Moment 2 — Lunch *(12:30–1:00pm)*

**What happens:**
Arjun eats the home-cooked meal from the morning instruction. It's different from yesterday. It's something he actually chose (via the app's suggestion) rather than defaulted to.

**Feeling:** Quietly satisfied. No guilt — he knows the meal was intentional and nutritionally reasonable.

---

### Moment 3 — Evening snack *(4:00–5:00pm)*

**What happens:**
If Arjun checks the app, it might surface a light snack idea — fruit, nuts, a protein option. He doesn't have to act on it, but it's there if he wants a nudge.

**Feeling:** The product earns passive trust here — it's useful without being demanding.

---

### Moment 4 — Dinner *(8:30–9:30pm)*

**What happens:**
The cook left dinner — a different dish from lunch, suggested by the app that morning. Arjun eats it without opening Swiggy. On nights when there's nothing, the app surfaces a specific delivery suggestion — one restaurant, one dish, matched to his goals — instead of the full Swiggy catalogue.

**Feeling:** The worst moment of the current journey becomes neutral or even good. No scrolling. No guilt. The decision was made hours ago.

## Where the Product Intervenes

| Intervention | Timing | What the product does | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning suggestion** | Before 7:15am | Surfaces today's meal based on fridge state, goals, preferences | Dish name + cook instruction (WhatsApp-ready) |
| **Dinner fallback** | By 7:00pm | Surfaces a specific delivery suggestion if no dinner is planned | Restaurant + dish + reason it fits goals |
| **Weekly grocery nudge** | Sunday evening | Suggests a varied grocery list for the coming week | Simple list tied to planned meals |
| **Passive progress** | Anytime | Shows a lightweight weekly nutrition summary — no logging required | "You've had good protein 4 days this week" |

---

## Key Interaction Principles (from journey)

1. **Push, don't pull.** The suggestion should arrive before Arjun opens the app — not require him to seek it out.
2. **One tap to act.** The cook instruction must be copy-paste or share-ready. No editing, no translating.
3. **Disappear after the decision.** Once Arjun acts on the suggestion, the product has no more job to do for that meal. Don't add steps.
4. **Earn the dinner moment.** The app that relieves the worst moment (dinner ordering) earns the most trust. This is the stickiness driver.
5. **Never ask for daily input.** The journey must work even if Arjun hasn't opened the app in 3 days.
