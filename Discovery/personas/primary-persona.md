# Primary Persona — Food Decision Sprint
**Source:** 5 user interviews + pre-interview surveys (August 2026)  
**Participants represented:** P1, P2, P3, P4, P5 — composite  
**Date compiled:** August 2026

---

## Arjun, 28
**Senior Analyst · Lives alone in Bangalore · Has a cook**

> *"I know what I should eat. I just never have the energy to figure it out when I need to."*

---

## Context

Arjun moved to Bangalore for work three years ago. He lives alone in a 1BHK, works a 9-to-6 office job, and commutes four days a week. He has a cook who comes every morning at 7:30am to prepare lunch and dinner. On busy nights, he falls back on Swiggy or Zomato. On weekends, he eats out with friends.

He cares about his health — he goes to the gym irregularly, tracks his protein intake loosely, and has done a yearly health checkup for the last two years. He is not a foodie in the obsessive sense, but he enjoys good food. He describes his relationship with food as somewhere between "easygoing" and "needs work."

---

## A Typical Weekday

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| **7:00am** | Wakes up, cook arrives shortly after |
| **7:15am** | Needs to tell the cook what to make — usually blanks, repeats yesterday or says "make whatever's there" |
| **7:30am** | Skips breakfast or has something light — cornflakes, fruit, coffee |
| **1:00pm** | Eats lunch at the office canteen or from the packed home-cooked meal |
| **4:00pm** | Gets hungry, has tea and a snack at the office |
| **8:30pm** | Gets home, eats what the cook left — or opens Swiggy if nothing feels right |
| **9:00pm** | Sometimes feels vaguely guilty about what he ate; occasionally asks ChatGPT if something was healthy |

---

## Goals

- Eat healthy at least 5 days a week without it becoming a project
- Not repeat the same 3 dishes every week
- Feel good about his food choices without obsessing over them
- Build a body that reflects the effort he puts into the gym

---

## Frustrations

- **Every morning is the same blank.** The cook arrives and Arjun has nothing prepared to say. He defaults to whatever vegetable is in the fridge — usually the same one from last week.
- **Too many options, too little guidance.** When he opens Swiggy hungry, he scrolls for 15 minutes and ends up ordering something he half-regrets.
- **Apps feel like homework.** He tried Healthify Me. He lasted two weeks before the daily logging became another obligation he was failing at.
- **Healthy food at restaurants is often junk in disguise.** He's been burned enough times ordering "healthy" options that were overpriced, tasteless, or both.
- **The cook is capable of more — but Arjun can't unlock it.** The cook will make whatever she's told. The bottleneck is always Arjun not knowing what to ask for.

---

## Behaviours

- Uses Swiggy/Zomato 3–4 times a week when home food falls short
- Has tried at least one calorie tracking app — abandoned within weeks
- Uses ChatGPT or Claude occasionally to ask food questions ("is this healthy?", "what can I make with these ingredients?")
- Gets food inspiration from YouTube and Instagram but never translates it into a plan
- Does yearly health checkups and makes dietary changes based on results (cut oil, cut sugar) — but these are reactive, not proactive
- Orders groceries online — defaults to the same 4–5 vegetables every week

---

## Relationship with Food

> *"We both know we can do better."*

Arjun doesn't hate food — he actually enjoys eating. But the daily friction of deciding, the guilt of defaulting, and the boredom of repetition have made food feel more like a chore than a pleasure. He knows the gap between what he eats and what he wants to eat. He just hasn't found anything that closes it without demanding more from him than he has to give.

---

## What Arjun Needs

| Need | In his words |
|---|---|
| A ready answer every morning | *"I like this idea of not having to think about what to eat every day, especially on those days when I am busy."* — P3 |
| Something he can hand straight to the cook | *"If that part could be tackled — what I should eat, correlated to what's possible — nothing like it."* — P2 |
| Flexibility, not a rigid plan | *"It has to be flexible. It also depends on my mood."* — P3 |
| To feel good, not tracked | *"It was guilt free because I knew it was home cooked."* — P4 |
| A shortcut, not a tool | *"AI is like very on demand. I don't have to do it every single day."* — P1 |

---

## What Arjun Does NOT Want

- Another app that requires daily logging
- A diet plan so rigid it breaks the moment the cook makes something different
- Generic suggestions that don't account for what's actually in his fridge
- Macro breakdowns and calorie counts front and centre
- A product that makes him feel surveilled or failing

---

## Design Implications

| Implication | Rationale |
|---|---|
| The app must have a ready suggestion before Arjun opens it | The decision moment is 7:15am — half-awake, cook waiting. No time for configuration. |
| Output must be a dish name + brief cook instruction, not a meal plan | Arjun's primary action is forwarding a message to the cook. The format must match that. |
| Onboarding must be once-only | Any recurring setup will be abandoned. Preferences, goals, and pantry staples are captured once and updated rarely. |
| Tone must feel like a knowledgeable friend, not a nutrition app | Arjun already uses AI for food questions because it feels conversational and non-judgmental. |
| Variety must be built in automatically | Arjun won't ask for variety — the system must rotate suggestions without being told to. |
