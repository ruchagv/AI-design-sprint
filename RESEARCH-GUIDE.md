# Research Guide — Food Decision Sprint

**Purpose:** Two-part guide — a pre-interview questionnaire to send before sessions, and an in-interview discussion guide.  
**Reference:** See [PROJECT-OVERVIEW.md](./PROJECT-OVERVIEW.md) for full context.

---

## What We're Trying to Learn

Every question in this guide serves one of three research goals:

| # | Research Goal | What We're Trying to Settle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Core problem** | Is the real pain about nutrition — or about the mental effort of deciding? |
| 2 | **Current behaviour** | How do people solve this today, and where do their workarounds break down? |
| 3 | **Opportunity size** | Is this painful enough that people would change behaviour and pay for a solution? |

---

## Participant Profile

Recruit participants who match **all** of the following:

- Urban professional in a metro city (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai)
- 9-to-5 or similar corporate job, commutes to office
- **Living alone** — single-person household
- Has a cook at home, **or** cooks themselves at least 3–4 days/week
- Eats at least 2 meals at home on a typical weekday
- Age: 25–40

**Aim for diversity across:**
- People with a dietary restriction (health, fitness goal, or religious)
- People who have tried and abandoned a diet/health app
- Mix of "has a cook" and "cooks themselves"

**Exclude:**
- People living with family, a partner, or flatmates — food decisions in shared households are out of scope for this round
- People who eat out for every meal
- People currently working with a dietician or nutritionist on a structured plan

## Part 1 — Pre-Interview Questionnaire

**When to send:** 24–48 hours before the interview  
**Format:** Google Form or Typeform — keep it under 5 minutes  
**Purpose:** Warm participants up, segment them, and save interview time on basics

---

**Q1.** What city do you live in?  
*(Open text)*

- Every day (5 days)

- Sometimes (1–2 days)

**Q3.** Who typically prepares your meals at home?
*(Select all that apply)*

## Part 2 — Interview Discussion Guide

**Session length:** 50–60 minutes  
**Format:** Video call or in-person  
**Team setup:** One designer facilitates, one takes notes  
**Note:** Review the participant's pre-interview questionnaire answers before the session — use them to tailor your probes

---

### Before You Start (Read Aloud)

> "Thanks for taking the time. We're exploring a product idea and trying to understand people's real experiences with food — not test you in any way. There are no right or wrong answers. The most useful thing you can do is be honest, even if that means saying something feels obvious or boring. I may ask follow-up questions just to understand better — it doesn't mean you said something wrong. Is it okay if I record this for our notes?"

---

### Warm-Up — 5 min

> "Before we dive in — just tell me about yesterday. Walk me through your day from morning to evening, and wherever food comes up, just mention it."

*Listen for:* energy levels, who's involved in food decisions, friction moments, moments of enjoyment  
*Don't redirect yet — just let them talk*

---

### Theme 1 — Decision Fatigue vs. Nutrition *(Core Research Goal 1)*

**Goal:** Understand whether the primary pain is "I don't know what's healthy" or "I'm too tired to figure out what to eat."

---

**Q1.** When you think about food stress in your life — what does it actually feel like? Is it more *"I don't know what I should eat"* or *"I'm too tired to think about it"* — or something else entirely?

*Listen for:* which framing resonates more — knowledge gap vs. energy/effort gap

---

**Q2.** Think about a recent moment when deciding what to eat felt genuinely difficult. What was going on? What made it hard at that moment?

*Probe if needed:*
- "Was it a health/nutrition question, or more just the effort of deciding?"
- "What did you end up doing?"
- "How did you feel about that decision afterwards?"

---

**Q3.** *(If they have dietary restrictions — from pre-questionnaire)* You mentioned [restriction]. How does that factor into daily decisions? Does it make things easier because it narrows your choices, or harder because it limits options?

*Listen for:* whether restrictions feel like a burden or a helpful constraint

---

**Q4.** *(Hypothetical)* Imagine someone handed you a perfectly planned weekly menu — healthy, fits your preferences, ready to go. Would that feel like a relief or would something about it bother you?

*Listen for:* need for control, trust issues, fear of boredom, delight at the idea

---

### Theme 2 — Current Workarounds *(Core Research Goal 2)*

**Goal:** Understand exactly how people solve this today — what works, what breaks, and what the gaps are.

---

**Q5.** Right now, without any app or system, how do you actually manage food decisions day-to-day? Walk me through what happens in a typical week.

*Listen for:* informal systems (mental rotations, default dishes, ordering patterns), how much cognitive load they personally carry, where the workaround breaks down

---

**Q6.** *(If they have a cook — from pre-questionnaire)* How do you tell your cook what to make? How far in advance? What happens on days when you haven't figured out what you want in time?

*Probe if needed:*
- "Does it feel like a chore to decide and then communicate that to your cook?"
- "Have you ever wished that decision could just be handled for you entirely?"

---

**Q7.** You mentioned you [tried an app / use an app / never tried one — from pre-questionnaire]. Tell me more about that.

*If tried and stopped:*
- "What was the moment you decided to stop?"
- "Was there anything you actually liked about it before you quit?"
- "What would have had to be different for you to keep using it?"

*If never tried:*
- "What's stopped you? Is it that you don't think it would help, or something else?"

---

**Q8.** Outside of apps — do you get food ideas or guidance from anywhere? (Social media, family, habit, restaurants you like, something else?)

*Listen for:* passive vs. active information-seeking; what triggers a "good food idea"

---

**Q9.** On your worst food days — the ones where you feel worst about what you ate — what typically went wrong? And on your best food days, what went right?

*Note: since they live alone, all of this is entirely self-directed — probe for how much of it is internal (mood, energy, motivation) vs. external (no groceries, long day, etc.)*

*Listen for:* what "good" actually means to them — healthy? satisfying? effortless? social?

---

### Theme 3 — Opportunity & Openness *(Core Research Goal 3)*

**Goal:** Gauge pain severity, openness to a new solution, and early willingness-to-pay signals.

---

**Q10.** On a scale of 1–10, how much of a problem is food decision-making in your daily life? And how does it compare to other stress in your day?

*Listen for:* relative priority — is this a 2/10 annoyance or a real daily burden?

---

**Q11.** If an app could give you personalised suggestions for what to eat — for all your meals — and it required almost no daily input from you, would you use it? What would make you trust it enough to actually follow its suggestions?

*Probe if needed:*
- "Would you need to be able to customise or override suggestions?"
- "What would make a suggestion feel 'right' vs. 'off'?"

---

**Q12.** *(Willingness-to-pay — ask directly, don't soften it)* If something like this existed and actually worked for you — how much would you be willing to pay per month for it?

*After they answer:*
- "What would you need to see in the product to feel that price was worth it?"
- "Is there a version of this you'd only use if it was free?"

---

### Wrap-Up — 5 min

**Q13.** Is there anything about your daily food life that you feel like we didn't cover — something that really shapes how you think about eating?

**Q14.** If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about food in your daily life — just one thing — what would it be?

*This is often the most revealing question. Give them space to answer.*

## Synthesis — What to Do After All Interviews

### Step 1 — Cluster observations by research goal

Create three columns on your FigJam/Miro board:

```
[ Core Problem ]     [ Current Workarounds ]     [ Opportunity Signal ]
```

Place each observation card in the most relevant column.

### Step 2 — Answer these three questions with evidence

1. **Is the core problem nutrition or decision fatigue?**  
   Tally: how many participants described a *knowledge gap* vs. an *effort/energy gap* as their primary frustration?

2. **How do people solve this today?**  
   List the top 3–5 workarounds you heard. For each: what works about it, and what breaks?

3. **Is this a real opportunity?**  
   - What % of participants said they'd use a solution like this?
   - What % named a price they'd pay?
   - What conditions did people attach to adoption?
