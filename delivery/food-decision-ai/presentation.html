The Problem
Karan knows what he wants to eat.
He has no system that converts what he knows
into an answer waiting for him.
Existing apps fail because
  • They ask him to log every meal
  • They require daily decisions
  • They treat him as a patient, not a professional
Our design principle
  • Decision made before he wakes up
  • Zero daily input after onboarding
  • Cook handoff costs him nothing

Sprint Learnings

What we learned building this

AI design sprint · key takeaways
🎯
The cook relationship is the product's superpower
Every other meal app ignores the domestic cook dynamic. That's the insight that makes MealCraft different — and it only surfaced through deep persona work.
🌅
Design for 7am, not 7pm
The use context — barely awake, one hand, under 10 seconds — forced every interaction decision. Warm light theme, large targets, zero cognitive load. Context is the brief.
✂️
Removing features is harder than adding them
The instinct was to add ratings, logging, and check-ins. Every one was cut. The zero-input promise only holds if you defend it aggressively from the start.
⚡
AI accelerated the sprint — but didn't replace the thinking
From UX direction to working prototype in one sprint. AI handled execution; the design judgment, persona work, and constraint-setting remained human.
